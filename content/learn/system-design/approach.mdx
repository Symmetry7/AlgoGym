## The idea

A system-design round is not a quiz with one right answer. The interviewer wants to watch you turn a vague product into a working design, make trade-offs out loud and change course when the requirements shift. A fixed plan keeps you from freezing or jumping straight into databases.

Use the same five steps every time, and say each one aloud so the interviewer can follow:

1. **Clarify requirements** (about 5 minutes)
2. **Estimate the scale** (about 5 minutes)
3. **Sketch the simplest design that works** (about 10 minutes)
4. **Go deep on two or three parts** (about 20 minutes)
5. **Wrap up** with bottlenecks and what you'd do next (about 5 minutes)

## Step 1: clarify requirements

Split them into two lists and write both on the board.

- **Functional**: what the system does. For a photo-sharing app: upload a photo with a caption, follow people, see a feed of people you follow. Pick the three or four that matter and say which you are leaving out ("I'll skip comments and stories unless you want them").
- **Non-functional**: how well it must do it. Who uses it, how many, from where; how fast the feed must load; whether a lost post is acceptable; whether a slightly stale feed is fine.

Good questions sound like *"Is this read-heavy? I'd guess people scroll far more than they post"* or *"Should a new post show up in followers' feeds within seconds, or is a minute fine?"* Each answer removes a branch of the design.

## Step 2: estimate the scale

Numbers turn "it should scale" into decisions. Keep the maths rough and say your assumptions.

Suppose 1 crore (10 million) daily users, each opening the feed 10 times and posting once every 10 days.

| What | Rough number |
| --- | --- |
| Feed reads a day | 10 million × 10 = 10⁸ |
| Feed reads a second | 10⁸ / 10⁵ s ≈ 1,000 on average, 3,000 at peak |
| New posts a day | 10 million / 10 = 10⁶, about 12 a second |
| Photo storage a day | 10⁶ × 2 MB = 2 TB, so about 700 TB a year |

Two useful facts fall out at once: reads outnumber writes about 100 to 1, so caching the feed will pay off; and storage grows by terabytes a day, so images belong in object storage, not in the database.

The trick that makes this fast: **a day is about 10⁵ seconds**. One million requests a day is about 12 a second.

## Step 4: go deep

Pick the two or three parts that are hardest for *this* product, or follow the interviewer's hints. For the photo app that's usually:

- **The feed.** Build it when it is read (query the people you follow and merge their posts) or when a post is written (push the post into each follower's precomputed feed)? Writing ahead is fast to read but expensive for accounts with lakhs of followers. Many designs mix both.
- **The read path.** Cache the hot feeds and post rows; serve images from a CDN.
- **Storage growth.** How the posts table is partitioned once it outgrows one machine.

For each deep dive, name the options, pick one, and give the reason in one sentence. "I'd use fan-out on write for normal users and fan-out on read for celebrities, because writing to 50 lakh feeds per post is too slow."

## Step 5: wrap up

Spend the last minutes on what would break next and what you would measure: the slowest query, the busiest cache key, the single machine whose failure takes the site down. Mention monitoring and how you'd roll out a change safely. It shows you think past the whiteboard.

## What interviewers look for

- **Structure.** You drive the conversation through clear phases instead of waiting to be asked.
- **Requirements before solutions.** You ask before you draw, and you write down what is in and out of scope.
- **Numbers behind decisions.** A rough estimate justifies each component. "We need a cache because…" beats "let's add Redis".
- **Trade-offs, named.** Every choice has a cost; say it. There is rarely one correct design.
- **Communication.** You think aloud, check in ("Does this match what you had in mind?") and take hints without getting defensive.

## Common mistakes

- **Jumping to technology names.** "Kafka, Cassandra, Kubernetes" in minute two, before anyone knows what the system does.
- **Designing for Google scale by default.** If the answer is 50 requests a second, one server and one database are a correct answer. Say so.
- **Going silent.** Long pauses while you think are fine, but say what you are weighing.
- **Spending 20 minutes on one box.** Get a whole working sketch first; depth comes after.
